The service

What water heater installation involves

Replacing a water heater is more than swapping one tank for another. A proper installation makes sure your new unit is the right size for your household, connected safely, and set up to last as long as it should.

Whether it’s an emergency water heater replacement or a planned upgrade, every installation we do in New Milford and the surrounding towns covers the same essentials:

  • Sizing the new unit to your household’s hot water demand
  • Draining and disconnecting the old water heater
  • New water connections and shut-off valves as needed
  • Electrical connections for your fuel type
  • Temperature & pressure relief valve and discharge line
  • Expansion tank where your plumbing system calls for one
  • Filling, testing and checking every connection for leaks
  • Setting a safe, efficient water temperature

Signs your water heater needs repair or replacement

Most tank-style water heaters last roughly 8–12 years. Hard water and well water sediment can shorten that. These are the warning signs we see most often:

Running out of hot water

Showers go cold sooner than they used to, or the tank takes a long time to recover between uses.

Rusty or cloudy water

Discolored hot water can mean the inside of the tank or the anode rod is corroding.

Popping or rumbling

Noises from the tank usually mean sediment has built up on the bottom, which wastes energy and stresses the tank.

Water around the base

Moisture or pooling near the tank can be a fitting or relief valve, or a failing tank. Have it checked quickly.

Your tank is 10+ years old

Older tanks fail without much warning. Planning a replacement beats an emergency on a cold morning.

Rising energy bills

An aging or sediment-filled water heater works harder to deliver the same hot water.

Water heater repair or replacement? Parts like heating elements, thermostats, gas controls and relief valves can often be repaired. A leak from the tank itself cannot, so that means replacement. We will tell you honestly which makes sense.

Choosing the right type of water heater

The best water heater for your home depends on your fuel source, space, budget and how much hot water you use. These are the main options we will walk you through:

Most common

Tank water heaters

Gas, propane or electric tanks that keep a supply of hot water ready to use.

  • Lowest upfront cost
  • Simple, quick replacement
  • Sized by gallons and first-hour rating
Endless hot water

Tankless water heaters

Heats water only when you open a tap, so it never runs out and takes up far less space.

  • Continuous hot water
  • Wall-mounted, saves floor space
  • Typically longer lifespan than a tank
Boiler homes

Indirect-fired water heaters

Common in Connecticut homes with oil or gas boilers. The boiler heats your water through a coil in an insulated storage tank.

  • Uses your existing boiler
  • Large hot water capacity
  • Long-lasting stainless tanks
High efficiency

Heat pump (hybrid electric)

Pulls heat from the surrounding air to heat water, using much less electricity than a standard electric tank.

  • Lowest operating cost for electric homes
  • Rebates may be available through Energize Connecticut
  • Needs space and airflow around the unit

Project considerations

What to consider before you replace

A few details make a big difference to cost, performance and how long your new water heater lasts.

Size & demand
The number of people, bathrooms and appliances in your home determines the tank size or tankless flow rate you need. Connecticut’s cold incoming water in winter matters for tankless sizing in particular.
Fuel & venting
Staying with the same fuel keeps costs down. Switching from electric to gas, or tank to tankless, may need a new gas line, venting or electrical work.
Space & location
Tank units need floor space and clearance; tankless units mount on a wall. Hard-to-reach basements and crawl spaces can affect the installation.
Water quality
Hard water and well water sediment shorten a water heater’s life. A water softener or filtration system can protect your new unit.
Safety & code
A properly piped relief valve, correct venting and, on many systems, an expansion tank are part of a safe, code-compliant installation.
Efficiency & rebates
Higher-efficiency units cost more up front but less to run. Check Energize Connecticut for current rebates, especially on heat pump water heaters.
