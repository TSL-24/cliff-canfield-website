The service

What a bathroom remodel involves

The plumbing is the part of a bathroom remodel you never see once the walls are closed, and the part that matters most. Getting it right means no leaks behind new tile, strong water pressure and drains that work for decades.

Whether you are refreshing fixtures or gutting the room to the studs, a bathroom remodel typically includes:

  • Planning the layout and fixture locations with you
  • Removing old fixtures and outdated piping
  • New water supply lines (often PEX) and shut-off valves
  • Drain, waste and vent piping sized and sloped correctly
  • Shower valves with scald protection and tub installation
  • Vanity, sink, faucet and toilet installation
  • Pressure-testing everything before walls are closed
  • Final fixture setup, testing and cleanup

Common problems

Signs it’s time to renovate your bathroom

Many bathroom remodels start with a plumbing problem. These are the issues we see most often in older Connecticut homes:

Leaks and water damage

Soft floors, stained ceilings below or loose tiles usually mean water has been getting where it shouldn’t.

Old galvanized or failing pipes

Corroded steel or patched copper causes low pressure and rusty water, and is best replaced while walls are open.

Shower temperature swings

Older valves without pressure balancing can scald when someone flushes. Modern valves fix that.

Slow or noisy drains

Undersized or poorly vented drains gurgle and back up. A remodel is the time to correct them.

Outdated or worn fixtures

Dripping faucets, cracked tubs and inefficient toilets waste water and money.

A layout that doesn’t work

Moving a toilet, adding a shower or a second sink means moving supply and drain lines.

Project considerations

What to plan for before your bathroom renovation

A little planning up front keeps a bathroom remodel on budget and on schedule.

Layout changes
Keeping fixtures where they are costs less. Moving a toilet or shower means rerouting drains, which depends on your floor structure and access.
Piping material
Remodels are the best time to replace old galvanized or failing copper. PEX is a common, durable choice for new supply lines.
Fixture selection
Choose fixtures early. Valve bodies and drains are installed before walls close, and some fixtures need specific rough-in measurements.
Water quality
Hard water and well water sediment are hard on new fixtures. Filtration or a softener protects your investment.
Permits & inspections
Most bathroom remodels involving new plumbing need a permit and inspection in Connecticut towns.
Timeline
Plumbing happens in stages: rough-in before walls close, finish plumbing after tile and paint. Plan for both visits.
