The service

What water filtration and treatment involves

The right water filtration system starts with knowing what is actually in your water. Many homes in our area are on private wells, and well water can change from one street to the next.

A typical water treatment installation includes:

  • Reviewing your water test results and concerns
  • Recommending the right equipment for your water
  • Installing filters, softeners or treatment units
  • Bypass valves so you can service the system easily
  • Drain and electrical connections where needed
  • Setting up and programming the system
  • Checking pressure and flow after installation
  • Explaining filter changes and maintenance

Common problems

Signs you need water treatment

Your water usually tells you when something is off. These are the signs we hear about most often:

White scale and spots

Crusty buildup on faucets and glassware means hard water, which also shortens the life of water heaters.

Orange or brown stains

Rust-colored stains in sinks, tubs and toilets point to iron, common in Connecticut well water.

Blue-green stains

Stains around drains can mean acidic, low-pH water that slowly eats away at copper pipes.

Sediment or cloudy water

Grit in aerators and cloudy water often come from the well and wear out fixtures.

Bad taste or odor

A chlorine taste on city water or a sulfur smell on well water are both treatable.

Dry skin and dull laundry

Hard water makes soap less effective and leaves residue on skin, hair and clothes.

Test first. For well water, a test from a state-certified lab tells you exactly what you are dealing with, including things you can’t see or taste. We will help you choose equipment based on those results.

Project considerations

What to consider before installing water treatment

A few details decide which system makes sense and how well it works.

Your water source
Private wells and city water have different problems. Well owners are responsible for their own water quality and testing.
Test results
Treating the wrong problem wastes money. Hardness, iron, pH and other results determine the right equipment.
Household size
Systems are sized to how much water your home uses. Undersized equipment loses pressure and wears out sooner.
Space & connections
Most systems install near where water enters the home and may need a drain and an outlet nearby.
Maintenance
Filters need changing and softeners need salt. Choose a system you are comfortable maintaining.
Protecting your home
Treating hard or acidic water protects your water heater, pipes and new fixtures from early failure.
