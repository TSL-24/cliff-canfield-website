The service

What gas line installation involves

Gas work has no margin for error. A proper gas line installation is sized for every appliance it serves, built with approved materials and tested for leaks before anything is lit.

Whether you use natural gas or propane, gas line work typically includes:

  • Calculating demand for every gas appliance
  • Sizing pipe for the length of the run and the load
  • Running new gas piping to the appliance location
  • Shut-off valves at each appliance
  • Appliance connections and sediment traps
  • Pressure-testing the new piping for leaks
  • Checking appliance operation after connection
  • Permits and inspection where required

When to call

Signs you need gas line work

Most gas line projects come from one of these situations:

Adding a gas appliance

A new range, dryer, fireplace or grill needs its own properly sized line and shut-off valve.

Switching to tankless

Tankless water heaters often need a larger gas line than the old tank did.

Installing a generator

Standby generators need a dedicated gas supply sized for their load.

Remodeling a kitchen

Moving or adding a range means moving or extending the gas line before walls and cabinets go in.

Old or corroded piping

Rusted or outdated gas piping should be inspected and replaced before it becomes a problem.

Appliances underperforming

Weak flames or appliances that won’t stay lit can point to undersized or restricted gas lines.

Smell gas? Don’t use switches, phones or flames inside. Leave the building right away and call your gas supplier or 911 from outside. Once it’s safe, we can repair the line.

Project considerations

What to consider before adding a gas line

These details decide how the gas line is designed and what it costs.

Natural gas or propane
Many homes in our area use propane from a tank rather than utility gas. Appliances and piping are set up for the fuel you have.
Total demand
Every gas appliance adds to the load. A line that worked for a stove may be too small once a tankless heater is added.
Distance & routing
The length of the run and how the pipe gets there affect pipe size, labor and cost.
Supply capacity
Your meter, regulator or propane setup must be able to supply the new load. Your gas supplier may need to be involved.
Permits & inspection
New gas piping requires a permit and inspection in Connecticut towns.
Ventilation
Gas appliances need proper venting and combustion air. Planning for it up front avoids problems later.
