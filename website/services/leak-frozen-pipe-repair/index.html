The service

What leak and frozen pipe repair involves

A frozen or leaking pipe needs fast, careful work. The goal is to stop the water, find exactly where the problem is and repair it with as little damage to your home as possible.

Every leak or frozen pipe repair includes:

  • Helping you shut off the water safely
  • Locating the frozen section or leak
  • Thawing frozen pipes safely, without open flame
  • Repairing or replacing damaged pipe and fittings
  • Working without opening walls whenever possible
  • Restoring water and checking for other leaks
  • Finding why it froze or leaked
  • Recommending fixes to prevent it next time

Common problems

Signs of a leaking or frozen pipe

Catch these early and you can avoid serious water damage:

No water on a cold morning

If one faucet or the whole house has no water during a cold snap, a pipe has likely frozen.

Frost or bulging on a pipe

Visible frost or a bulge means the pipe is frozen and may already be split.

Water stains or soft drywall

Stains on ceilings or walls and soft spots usually mean a hidden leak.

Running water sounds

Hearing water when nothing is on can point to a leak inside a wall or floor.

Higher water bills or a busy well pump

A pump that runs more than usual or a jump in your bill can mean water is leaking somewhere.

Blue-green stains on copper

Corrosion on copper pipes, often from acidic well water, can lead to pinhole leaks.

If a pipe bursts: turn off your main water shut-off right away, turn off power to anything electrical near the water if it’s safe, and call us. Never thaw a pipe with an open flame.

Prevention

How to prevent frozen and leaking pipes

Connecticut winters are hard on plumbing. These steps make a big difference:

Exposed pipes
Pipes in crawl spaces, garages, attics and outside walls freeze first. Insulating them is the simplest protection.
Outdoor spigots
Disconnect hoses in fall and shut off and drain outdoor spigots, or upgrade to frost-free sillcocks.
Cold snaps
On the coldest nights, open cabinet doors on outside walls and let a faucet drip to keep water moving.
Away from home
Keep the heat on when you travel, and know how to shut off your main water valve.
Water quality
Acidic well water slowly thins copper pipe. Treating it prevents pinhole leaks.
Know your shut-off
Find and test your main shut-off now, before you need it in an emergency.
